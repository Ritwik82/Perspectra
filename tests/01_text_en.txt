The gender pay gap is real but people keep arguing about the wrong thing.
So here's my position. The raw gender pay gap is a useful indicator of economic inequality, but it is not a direct measurement of how much employers discriminate against women for doing the same work. To establish that kind of discrimination, we need more specific evidence. At the same time, comparing a handful of men and women with similar job titles isn't enough to establish that the labour market is fair.
Companies should publish clearer pay and promotion data, investigate unexplained disparities, and make their criteria for raises and promotions transparent. Those measures are useful whether the underlying causes turn out to be discrimination, differences in career paths, organisational practices, or some combination of them.
What annoys me is that people treat this as a contest where one side has to prove that women are universally disadvantaged and the other has to prove that discrimination never happens. Neither claim is necessary to justify examining the evidence.
We should stop treating a single pay-gap number as proof of a specific cause, but we should also stop treating the absence of a perfect causal estimate as proof that there's no problem worth fixing.
